How the competition works

Anatomy of a round

Unlike a combative debate, an Ethics Olympiad round unfolds in four civil, collaborative stages:

01

Ethical Presentation

5 min

Team A opens with a structured stance on an unannounced ethical case: naming the moral tension at its core, identifying the stakeholders involved, and applying a philosophical lens (utilitarian, deontological, virtue-based) to make their case.

02

Collaborative Commentary

2 min

The opposing team responds — not with a rebuttal, but with constructive commentary: what's the blind spot, what's been left out, where does the case deserve more nuance.

03

Response

3 min

The presenting team addresses that commentary head-on, sharpening, defending, or revising their position in real time.

04

Judges' Questions

Professional ethicists probe the case further, testing for clarity, rigor, and intellectual honesty — this is where the strongest teams actually stand out.

Teams then swap roles for a second case, and the process repeats across four rounds in total.

This is not a debate. Teams can agree with each other's position. You are not scored on winning an argument — you're scored on how clearly, honestly, and thoughtfully you engage with the ethical dimensions of the case.

Preparing for a round

You do not need a philosophy background to compete. Case packages are sent five weeks before the event, and the resources below cover the ethical frameworks, judging rubric, and case archive you'll want to work through beforehand:

  • Prep resource folder — case archives, primers, and practice materials for registered teams.
  • Modus Ponens Institute resources — the judging rubric, ethical framework guides, and coaching materials from our judging partner.
