#### Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment (Assessment A):** Mid-slop verdict — not an instant "AI made this," but it fails on second look, and it fails on exactly one tell, repeated everywhere: a 3px teal `border-left`/`border-top` stripe is the site's entire decorative vocabulary, applied identically across 6 selectors (`.format__theories li`, `.eligibility__list li`, `.timeline__list li`, `.results-row`, `.format__callout`, `.format__step`) for roughly 20 visible instances on one scroll. This is the textbook banned side-stripe pattern — the color does no semantic work anywhere; every stripe means the same thing on every item type. The font stack (Cinzel + Playfair Display + Cormorant Garamond + Inter) stacks four reflex-reject fonts in the single most predictable arrangement possible (display / subhead / one poetic line / body), and Cormorant Garamond appears exactly once on the entire page — dead weight both as a design decision and as a network request. The hero-metric template, gradient text, and glassmorphism-as-default are all genuinely absent — credit where due.

**Deterministic scan (Assessment B):** `detect.mjs` found 2 CLI-level findings against `index.html` (em-dash overuse: 26 instances; numbered-section-markers advisory) and 72 anti-patterns via the injected browser detector. The two most load-bearing categories **corroborate Assessment A's manual finding independently**: `border-accent-on-rounded` (4 instances) and `side-tab` (`border-left: 3px`, 1 instance) are the same side-stripe pattern A flagged by reading the CSS directly — deterministic and LLM judgment agree, which is the strongest possible signal on a finding. `overused-font` (Inter at 73% of text) also corroborates A's point about the other three fonts being decorative-only. The `ai-color-palette` finding ("cyan neon text on dark background," ~9 instances) is a real, if more debatable, hit — it's flagging the indigo→teal brand accent, which Assessment A judged as defensible inherited identity (matches the TKEthics logo's own gradient) rather than generic AI-purple/cyan. Worth knowing this reads as an AI-cliché color to an automated check even though it has a real provenance reason.

**False positive alert — the bulk of the raw finding count:** ~50 of the 72 browser findings are `low-contrast` checks computed against `#ffffff`. This site has no white backgrounds anywhere — it's a `color-scheme: dark` page with a near-black body (`#0E1013`) throughout. This is almost certainly the detector defaulting to a white background when it can't resolve an element's actual computed ancestor background, not a real finding. I'm discarding these and relying on Assessment A's smaller set of manually-verified, computed-against-real-background contrast failures instead (below, under Priority Issues) — those are real.

**Visual overlays:** Assessment B's browser injection succeeded and captured console output, but it ran in the sub-agent's own isolated browser tab, and it stopped its live server as part of cleanup per protocol — so there's no overlay currently visible in any tab you have open. #### Overall Impression

The writing is the best thing on this page, and it's good enough that the visual design's shortfalls are frustrating rather than fatal — this reads like a site where all the thinking went into the words and the CSS was assembled from the nearest available card-and-stripe components. The single biggest opportunity: today, this page's only job (convert a visitor into a signup) is structurally impossible, because both CTAs are correctly disabled and nothing replaces them. Fix that first; everything else is refinement.

#### What's Working

1. **The copy has a real point of view and never talks down.** "Forget everything you know about debate," "going head-to-head with ideas instead of insults," and the 90-word ethical-theories primer do more persuasive work than any layout choice on the page — and they're specifically aimed at defusing the objection ("I don't know philosophy") that kills signups in this category.
2. **Radical honesty in the microcopy builds real trust.** `Tentative`/`Estimated` tags, date-accurate CTA states via `getStageState` (no link that silently 404s), "cross-school teams are not recommended" — most competitors in this category fake certainty; this site doesn't, and a coach evaluating it will notice.
3. **The FAQ is real domain content.** Mic etiquette during private conferences, the head-judge tie-break procedure, the written-complaint process — these read as written by someone who has actually run a round, not filled in from a template.

#### Priority Issues

**[P0] No fallback action exists while both conversion paths are closed**
- **Why it matters:** The interest window opens 22 Sept, registration 6 Oct — correct, intentional. But the page offers zero alternative action in the meantime: no "notify me," no calendar export for 21 Nov, no mailto fallback near the disabled form. A visitor arriving from today's invitation email — which is most of the traffic, since email-blast traffic front-loads — has exactly one possible action: scroll to the bottom and manually copy one of six addresses.
- **Fix:** Swap the disabled interest form for a one-field "email me when it opens" capture (same Apps Script endpoint, a `notify-only` flag), add an `.ics` calendar download next to the hero date, and change the hero CTA's label to match reality ("Interest opens 22 Sept") rather than promising an action it can't deliver.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable onboard`

**[P1] The side-stripe accent is the site's entire decorative vocabulary — the banned pattern, confirmed by both assessments**
- **Why it matters:** Identical 3px teal borders on 6 different component types (theory cards, eligibility items, timeline rows, results rows, the callout, step cards) read as one long column of the same decorated rectangle repeated ~20 times. The color carries no meaning anywhere — this is exactly the "make the card look designed" reflex the absolute-ban rule targets, and it's the one thing both the LLM review and the deterministic scanner flagged independently.
- **Fix:** Reserve the accent stripe for the one place it's earned (`.format__callout`, a genuine pull-quote) and give the other five components distinct, purposeful treatments — full borders, background tints, a leading numeral/icon where it encodes real sequence, or nothing at all.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

**[P1] The sticky header occludes every anchor-jump target — the site's only navigation is broken**
- **Why it matters:** `style.css` defines no `scroll-padding-top`. Clicking any of the 9 nav links (verified: Format) lands the target `<h2>` at 22px while the sticky header's bottom sits at 65px — the heading is hidden on arrival. At 390px the header is 146px tall, hiding the heading *and* the first paragraph of every section. Every shared `#format`-style deep link is broken the same way.
- **Fix:** `html { scroll-padding-top: 88px }`, with a mobile media-query bump to match the taller wrapped header.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

**[P1] Real WCAG AA contrast failures (verified against actual computed backgrounds, not the detector's false-positive white-background sweep)**
- **Why it matters:** `--color-text-muted: #6B7280` measures 3.18:1 on `--color-bg-card` and 3.94:1 on `--color-bg-primary` — both fail the 4.5:1 body-text bar. This hits the footer, the tentative tag, all three "coming soon" placeholders, and the results-row school name. Separately, `.btn--primary`'s text (`#08090B`) sits at only 2.82:1 against the indigo end of its own gradient — the left half of "Express Interest" fails AA on the page's single most important button.
- **Fix:** Move muted text to a darker-against-its-actual-background value (test each context individually, don't reuse one gray everywhere), and either flatten the button to a single AA-safe color or set the text color per-stop with a gradient-aware technique.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

**[P1] The interest form stays fully fillable while submission is disabled**
- **Why it matters:** Verified in-browser: all 4 fields (`if-name`, `if-email`, `if-school`, `if-role`) are enabled while `interest-submit` is disabled, and the explanation ("The interest form opens 22 Sept 2026.") renders in `#interest-status` — after the button, after all four fields. A visitor can fill out the entire form and only then discover it goes nowhere; on mobile the explanation is a full scroll below the first field.
- **Fix:** Disable the whole fieldset in the `before`/`after` branches and move the status message above the first field, not below the button.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

#### Persona Red Flags

**Casey (distracted mobile user):** The 146px-tall wrapped header (no hamburger, 9 links across two rows) eats over 20% of an iPhone SE viewport on every screen. The timeline rows collapse — `min-width: 160px` on the date leaves ~110px for content, so "Express Interest form closes; Registration opens" wraps to 5 ragged lines beside a 1-line date. Every nav tap hides its own destination behind the header. The page is 10,732px tall with three consecutive "coming soon" sections stacked in the back half — Casey will not reach the FAQ, which is the section most likely to convert.

**Jordan (first-timer):** The hero's primary CTA promises an action ("Express Interest") and delivers a greyed-out button with no warning attached to the promise. Eligibility (age, team size, cost) sits below the entire Format section, so a student wondering "is this even for me?" reads ~1,500 words of mechanics first. There's no social proof anywhere — Judges, Gallery, and Results are all placeholders — so Jordan has no evidence this event has run before. Four of the nine nav items (Judges, Gallery, Results, arguably Timeline) lead to empty content: a 44% dead-link rate on the only navigation.

**Sam (accessibility-dependent):** `scroll-behavior: smooth` has no `prefers-reduced-motion` guard, forcing animated multi-thousand-pixel scrolls on users who've explicitly asked their OS for less motion. Zero `:focus`/`:focus-visible` rules exist anywhere in `style.css`. Form errors have no `aria-live`/`aria-describedby` wiring and never move focus to the first invalid field. No skip-link exists, so every anchor jump means re-traversing 9 nav links first. The TKEthics logo's tagline renders illegible at `height: 32px` in the header.

#### Minor Observations

- Heading line-height inherits the body's 1.65 with no override — measured 68.64px leading on 41.6px Cinzel. This is a large part of why the headings read as "configured" rather than "set": add `h1, h2, h3 { line-height: 1.12; text-wrap: balance }`.
- The `.format__steps` 4-up grid squeezes ~20-25 characters per line inside a 920px narrow container, breaking card 01's text across 3 ragged lines; the time-chip wraps inconsistently across cards 01/02 vs. 03, and card 04 has no time chip at all. Worth a dedicated `/impeccable layout` pass, or drop to a 2×2 grid.
- `#gallery` uses `.container` instead of `.container--narrow`, shifting its heading 160px left of every sibling section — likely an oversight, not a deliberate widen for future gallery grid needs.
- `.format__theories li:hover` sets border colors to values identical to their resting state — a hover rule that does nothing but add a box-shadow. Dead code.
- The honeypot field (aria-hidden wrapper, tabindex="-1", autocomplete="off", off-screen positioning) is genuinely well-built — worth calling out as a model for the rest of the form's accessibility work.
- Two competing "step" numbering systems on one page: the format section's earned 01-04 temporal sequence, and a second, unrelated "Step 1 —"/"Step 2 —" prose convention in the eligibility funnel. Pick one vocabulary.
- Six organizer emails are listed flat with no indication of who handles what — a coach with a billing question and a student with a rules question get the same undifferentiated list.
